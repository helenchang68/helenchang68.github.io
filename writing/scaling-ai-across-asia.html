The hardest part of scaling AI across Asia

The hardest part of scaling an AI product across Asia may have very little to do with the AI.

A product can work extremely well technically and still face very different paths to adoption in Singapore, Korea, Japan, or elsewhere in APAC.

The technology might be the same.

But the market around it is not.

Enterprise buying processes differ. Regulations differ. Local competitors differ. The partnerships that matter differ. Even the way companies establish trust with customers can vary significantly from one market to another.

Having worked across fintech, B2B technology and capital markets in Asia, this is one of the GTM questions I keep coming back to as I learn more about the AI industry.

A lot of attention understandably goes to model capabilities and product innovation.

But once the technology works, another set of questions begins.

And those questions are often much more market-specific than the technology itself.

  • Who needs it enough to actually pay for it?
  • Who will use it, and who ultimately decides whether to buy it?
  • What does a customer need to see before they are comfortable adopting it?
  • Which partnerships can make it easier to reach customers and deploy the product locally?
  • And what needs to be localized beyond the product itself?

As AI products become increasingly capable, I suspect these questions will become more important, not less.

For those working on bringing AI products to market across APAC, what has been the hardest part of going from “this works” to “customers here will actually adopt it”?
