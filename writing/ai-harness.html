What is an AI harness?

Over the weekend, I was at a friend's gathering when someone I had just met introduced himself by saying:

“I build harnesses around AI.”

I knew what he meant, but judging by the reactions around the room, quite a few people didn't.

It reminded me how quickly the vocabulary around AI is evolving. “Harness” is one of those terms I've been hearing much more often lately, but outside the AI space, it still seems unfamiliar to many.

So what exactly is an AI harness, and why is it becoming so important?

At its simplest, an AI harness, sometimes called an agent harness, is the system built around a model that helps it actually do useful work.

The model provides the intelligence.

The harness gives it things like:

  • context and memory
  • access to tools and APIs
  • permissions
  • instructions on what to do next
  • orchestration and execution loops
  • feedback and verification
  • guardrails and error handling

A useful shorthand is:

Agent = Model + Harness

Without the surrounding system, an LLM can generate an answer.

Give it tools, memory, an execution environment and a loop for deciding what to do next, and now it can start completing multi-step tasks.

What I find particularly interesting is what this means for how we think about AI products.

We spend a lot of time comparing models: which one is smarter, faster or better at reasoning?

But two products using the same underlying model can behave very differently depending on what has been built around it.

As models continue to improve, perhaps an increasingly important question will be:

Not just “Which model are you using?”

But “What has been built around it?”
