What is the AI actually doing?

At the recent Dell Technologies Forum Singapore 2026, a fireside chat featuring Diana Ser and Dr Chen Hui Ong got me thinking about an increasingly important question:

As AI becomes more autonomous, it is no longer enough to know whether the system is running.

We also need to know: What is the AI actually doing?

It made me think of it almost as “surveillance for AI.”

Looking into the concept afterward, the more established term seems to be AI observability, or more specifically for agents, agent observability.

If you've ever let AI handle a task for you and then thought, “Wait, what exactly did it just do?” you've already felt the need for observability.

With a simple chatbot, the interaction is relatively easy to see. You ask something, it responds.

But an AI agent might work through many steps on its own.

It could retrieve data, call APIs, use different tools, delegate work to another agent, make decisions and take actions before producing the final result.

So what happens when something goes wrong?

  • Which tool did it call?
  • What information did it use?
  • Where did it go off track?
  • How much did the entire task cost?
  • Did it access something it should not have?
  • Did it technically complete the task but arrive at the wrong outcome?

This is where observability becomes important.

If a harness gives an AI agent the environment, tools and structure it needs to operate, observability gives us visibility into how it actually operated.

And that distinction feels increasingly important as we move from AI that mostly answers questions to AI that can actually take action.

The more autonomy we give AI, the more important it becomes to understand what happens between the prompt and the final result.

Perhaps the next phase of enterprise AI is not only about making agents more capable.

It is also about making their actions more visible, traceable and easier to govern.
