Local context in customer-facing AI

One point that stayed with me from NiCE World 2026 Singapore last week was Mark Harington’s example of a very familiar sight in Singapore: using a packet of tissues to chope a seat.

Most people here would immediately understand what it means if they saw tissues left on a hawker centre table. Mark joked that when his wife tried the same thing in England, she came back to find other people sitting there, apparently quite happy with the tissues she had left behind.

It was a light example, but I thought it captured the idea of local context quite well.

It reminded me of Korean too, where even a simple phrase like 괜찮아요 (gwaenchanayo) can mean “it’s okay” or function as a polite “no thanks,” depending on the situation. The words themselves are only part of the message.

Much of the session focused on the orchestration behind AI agents and how they bring together customer context, information retrieval, reasoning, and different tools to complete a real customer journey rather than simply answer a question.

From a GTM perspective, I found it particularly relevant that some of that context can be very market-specific.

When customer-facing AI is deployed across different countries, localization is probably not just about translating the language. Expectations around tone, communication, escalation, trust, and service can also vary significantly.

This is something I have seen more broadly when working across Asian markets. The underlying product may be the same, but how customers understand, evaluate, and eventually adopt it can be quite different.

For B2B AI companies expanding across markets, that makes local context relevant not only to sales and marketing, but potentially to how the customer experience itself is designed.

Thanks, NiCE, Mark Harington, and Deepak Chandran for an insightful session at NiCE World 2026 Singapore.
