Delivery robots: getting onto the sidewalk is the hard part

I've seen delivery robots at COEX in Seoul, South Korea, picking up food from restaurants in the mall and delivering it to connected office buildings, but I've yet to see one operating on public sidewalks.

Yesterday, Serve Robotics announced that it's opening its US delivery robot network to developers, businesses, and even AI assistants through a public API.

With around 2,000 robots serving more than 150 US neighborhoods, Serve is making its fleet accessible beyond its existing delivery-platform partners. Through the API, businesses could request robots to deliver purchases from local shops or transport items between offices, without operating their own fleets.

Separately, Serve launched its first overseas robot delivery service on Australia's Gold Coast with DoorDash last month, starting with a small food-delivery pilot.

I was surprised to learn that Singapore is exploring autonomous deliveries too! Grab, DHL, and QuikBot are among the companies involved in a planned robotics testbed at Punggol Digital District, exploring applications including food and parcel delivery. Singapore is also preparing a regulatory sandbox for robots on public paths in early 2027.

Singapore's urban density could make short-distance robot deliveries attractive, but crowded footpaths, tropical weather, and navigating high-rise buildings present challenges.

And getting robots onto sidewalks isn't just about technology. I've seen videos on X of delivery robots being kicked, overturned, and vandalized in US cities. There are also concerns about robots replacing workers and broader anxiety about AI entering everyday life.

I understand the fear of losing jobs to automation. But I also remember seeing a motorcycle delivery rider get hit hard by a car in Seoul, and I've read tragic reports of delivery riders being injured or killed in Taiwan. With delivery work often being time-sensitive, these workers face real risks on the road.

I know many people depend on delivery work for a living, but I do hope robots can eventually take on some of the more dangerous deliveries, so fewer people have to face those risks.
