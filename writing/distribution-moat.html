Why a better AI product may not be enough

I used to wonder why companies would choose Copilot or Gemini when ChatGPT and Claude felt so much easier to use.

When AI assistants first started becoming popular, I was comparing the applications themselves, not the underlying models. I hadn't considered how much a company's existing software environment might influence its decision.

A company already using Microsoft 365 can introduce Copilot into familiar applications like Outlook, Word, and Teams. Google has a similar advantage with Gemini across Gmail, Docs and other Workspace applications.

An independent AI provider might offer a better experience for a particular task. But introducing it could mean another vendor approval, security review, system integration, and getting employees to adopt a new tool.

Microsoft and Google still need to demonstrate the value of their AI products. But existing customer relationships, purchasing arrangements and established workflows give them a head start.

I later came across the concept of a distribution moat.

It's not just about making existing customers reluctant to switch. An established software ecosystem can also make new products easier to introduce, sell, and roll out.

Of course, companies can use Copilot alongside ChatGPT or Claude, both of which are expanding their integrations with enterprise systems.

Specialized AI companies may also have an advantage when it comes to deeper capabilities or workflows that span multiple platforms.

Still, for an independent AI vendor, having a better product may not be enough. The improvement needs to justify the additional cost and effort of adopting it.

That also helps explain why partnerships, integrations, and marketplaces are so important in enterprise AI. They can help independent providers reach customers through systems those customers already use.

That leaves me curious about which enterprise problems are important enough to justify bringing in another AI vendor, even when a convenient option is already built into the tools employees use.
