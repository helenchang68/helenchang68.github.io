Is AI adoption paying off?

Companies are increasingly encouraged to adopt AI. But how much of that adoption is actually paying off?

McKinsey’s August 2026 global survey of 1,719 respondents across 97 countries offers a picture of AI’s progress across three dimensions.

  1. Adoption: Nearly 9 in 10 respondents reported regular AI use in at least one business function at their organizations.
  2. Individual productivity: 80% said AI had improved their own productivity.
  3. Financial impact: Yet only 37% reported that AI had contributed positively to their organization’s operating earnings.

A recent analysis by Apollo’s chief economist, Torsten Slok, takes an even more skeptical view.

He argues that AI has delivered “zero financial payoff” outside tech, pointing to largely flat revenue per worker and profit margins among the S&P 493, compared with improvements among the Magnificent Seven.

However, the S&P 493 includes companies at different stages of AI adoption, including potential non-adopters. The comparison suggests that broad financial gains aren’t yet visible in those measures, but doesn’t establish whether companies that have implemented AI are seeing returns.

Neither finding proves that AI adoption isn’t paying off. But they raise an important question about how AI adoption translates into financial results.

Businesses might use AI to serve more customers, generate additional revenue, or reduce operating costs by automating work and potentially reducing headcount. Yet productivity improvements alone don’t guarantee financial returns, especially when the costs of implementing and operating AI are taken into account.

For businesses expanding AI deployments, and vendors encouraging them to do so, demonstrating that connection between AI adoption and measurable financial value may matter more than showing how widely AI is being used.

If your company has adopted AI, what kind of payoff have you seen so far (time savings, cost reductions, additional revenue, etc.)?
