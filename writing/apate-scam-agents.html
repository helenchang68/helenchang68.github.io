Apate.ai: a win-win-win AI business built on fighting scams

In 2021, Dali Kaafar was enjoying a picnic with his family in Sydney when he received a scam call.

Instead of hanging up, he decided to play along, entertaining his children by keeping the scammer on the phone for 44 minutes. He also realized that every minute he kept the scammer talking was a minute they couldn't spend scamming someone else.

That experience sparked another thought. What if technology could do this at scale, while also turning these conversations into something useful?

That idea eventually became Apate.ai, an Australian startup using AI agents to fight scams.

Its agents pose as potential victims, engaging scammers through phone calls, messages, and online platforms. Using conversational AI, they keep scammers occupied while gathering valuable intelligence on their tactics, behavioral patterns, and the bank accounts or crypto wallets they use to collect stolen money.

According to the company, its agents have already conducted 2.5 million conversations, consuming a combined 17 years of scammers' time.

When I first heard about Apate, I genuinely thought, what a brilliant business idea! The more I looked into it, the more I appreciated how its business model works.

Rather than selling an app to consumers, Apate works with banks, telecom providers, and government agencies. Telecom partners can divert suspicious calls to its AI agents, while financial institutions use the intelligence collected to strengthen fraud prevention.

What I find particularly interesting is how it creates a potential win-win-win:

  • Consumers: benefit from better protection against scams.
  • Institutions: gain actionable intelligence to identify and disrupt fraud.
  • Apate: generates recurring revenue while building a proprietary dataset that can help improve its AI agents over time.

This also creates the potential for a self-reinforcing data flywheel:

  1. More scam interactions
  2. Richer intelligence
  3. Better AI agents
  4. Greater customer value
  5. More institutional adoption
  6. More scam interactions (and the cycle continues...)

I find this a fascinating example of how AI can create meaningful social impact while building a commercially sustainable business.
