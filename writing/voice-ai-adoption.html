From voice AI demo to adoption decision

ElevenLabs’ Singapore expansion got me looking more closely at how businesses choose and adopt voice AI.

I was curious about what goes into that decision beyond how natural the voice sounds, especially when it needs to work with existing systems and human teams.

ElevenLabs offers its own speech technology and an agent platform for connecting business tools, testing conversations and monitoring performance.

One detail in its documentation caught my attention. An ElevenLabs agent can use an OpenAI language model alongside ElevenLabs’ speech models. Choosing one does not necessarily mean excluding the other.

Two published customer stories made the commercial question clearer.

Meesho, an Indian e-commerce marketplace, built a voice agent using ElevenLabs’ text-to-speech technology for order and delivery questions in Hindi and English. Its case study highlights voice quality as a reason for choosing the provider.

Funding Societies, a Southeast Asian financing platform, uses ElevenLabs’ conversational AI for initial outreach and qualification before human agents take over. Its team prioritized voice quality, response speed, and ease of deployment.

Reading the two cases together, I became more interested in how the benefits would show up in the customer’s day-to-day operations.

For a similar support deployment, lower handling costs would be less convincing if customers kept calling back about the same issue. The cost of resolving the problem, including repeat contacts and human follow-up, would give a fuller picture.

For outreach, more conversations could be valuable, but the benefit would depend on whether human agents receive suitable prospects with enough context to continue the conversation. Otherwise, some of the time saved on initial calls could simply become extra screening work later.

These are questions a limited deployment could help investigate before a customer commits to a wider rollout, alongside the integration effort and ongoing costs.

Comparing the cases helped me think more concretely about how a prospective customer gets from an impressive demonstration to an adoption decision. Voice quality and response speed help explain why a provider gets considered. The next conversation is about which part of the customer’s process should improve and what evidence would justify investing further.
