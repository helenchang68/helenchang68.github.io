What Anthropic's own study shows about AI at work

By August, AI agents at Anthropic were logging six times as many work hours as its employees.

The figure comes from Anthropic's newly published study on how work is changing inside the company. Agent hours aren't equivalent to human hours, but the broader findings suggest this is becoming much more than a productivity story.

Even as technical hiring continued growing in absolute terms, the share of junior technical hires fell from 27% to 9%. Managers described needing fewer people for certain entry-level coding and bug-finding tasks as AI took on more of that work.

And the changes aren't limited to engineers.

A sales representative built a custom pipeline dashboard in just 3–4 hours, despite that kind of technical work being outside their usual skill set. Another reported that AI automations freed up enough time to speak with 30% more customers.

The researchers also observed that sales representatives closed deals with more clients after adopting AI agents, although not all of that increase can necessarily be attributed to AI.

As AI agents take on more work, human attention is becoming a bottleneck.

AI agents are generating so much output that, even for non-technical employees, reading everything would take more than half their workweek.

Employees are increasingly spending time directing agents, building dashboards, monitoring workflows, and deciding what actually needs their attention.

And this shift is already showing up in hiring priorities.

Anthropic's hiring managers are placing more emphasis on autonomy, judgment, and creative use of AI. For non-technical candidates, customer orientation is receiving greater attention relative to execution skills.

For commercial teams, this raises a question I find increasingly difficult to answer.

If salespeople can build tools and automate workflows themselves, where does that leave the rest of their work? With enough customer context, could AI also increasingly identify customer needs, prioritize opportunities, and recommend which actions to take?

The boundary between tasks we automate and decisions we reserve for people may be less fixed than we assume.

What stands out to me is how quickly that boundary is moving. According to Anthropic's report, AI is already changing how work is divided, what employees spend their time on, and the skills managers look for when hiring.

Anthropic is one of the companies shaping frontier AI capabilities while also deploying them throughout its own operations. What happens there may not translate directly to other organizations or on the same timeline, but it offers an early look at how work could evolve as similar capabilities become more widely adopted.
